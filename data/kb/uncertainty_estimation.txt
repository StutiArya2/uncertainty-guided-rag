Uncertainty estimation asks how confident a model is in its own output. Aleatoric uncertainty arises from noise inherent in the data, while epistemic uncertainty reflects gaps in what the model has learned. Common practical estimators include token-level log probabilities, disagreement across sampled generations, and ensemble variance. Retrieval systems can also derive a confidence signal from similarity scores and the margin between the top result and its runner-up. A large margin suggests the top result is decisively better, whereas a narrow margin suggests the ranking is unstable.
