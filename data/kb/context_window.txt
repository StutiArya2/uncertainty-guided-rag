The context window is the maximum number of tokens a language model can attend over in a single forward pass. Attention cost grows quadratically with sequence length, so long contexts are expensive in both time and memory. Retrieval-augmented systems consume context aggressively because every retrieved passage occupies space that could otherwise hold reasoning or conversation history. Models also exhibit a "lost in the middle" effect, attending less reliably to information placed in the centre of a long context. Shorter, better-targeted context often outperforms simply retrieving more.
