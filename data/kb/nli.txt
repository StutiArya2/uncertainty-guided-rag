Natural language inference classifies the relationship between a premise and a hypothesis as entailment, contradiction, or neutral. In fact-verification pipelines the retrieved evidence serves as the premise and the claim under test as the hypothesis. The probability assigned to the entailment class functions as a continuous support score rather than a binary judgment. NLI models are considerably smaller and faster than generative models, making them practical for scoring many claim-evidence pairs. The MultiNLI corpus is the standard training set for these models.
