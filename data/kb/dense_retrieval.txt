Dense retrieval encodes queries and documents into fixed-length vectors and ranks by similarity in that shared space. Unlike lexical methods it can match paraphrases that share no vocabulary, so a query about "car" can retrieve a passage about "automobile". Dense retrievers are typically trained with contrastive objectives that pull matching query-document pairs together and push mismatched pairs apart. Their main weakness is out-of-domain generalisation, where they often underperform BM25. Hybrid systems combine both signals to get the benefits of each.
