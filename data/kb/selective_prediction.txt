Selective prediction allows a model to decline to answer when its confidence falls below a threshold. The tradeoff is measured by a risk-coverage curve: coverage is the fraction of inputs answered, and risk is the error rate on those answers. Lowering the threshold raises coverage but usually raises risk as well. Abstention is valuable in high-stakes settings where a wrong answer costs more than no answer. A system that abstains well is more trustworthy than one with marginally higher raw accuracy.
