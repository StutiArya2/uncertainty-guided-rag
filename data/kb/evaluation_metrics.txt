Retrieval quality is commonly measured with recall at k, mean reciprocal rank, and normalised discounted cumulative gain. Recall at k asks whether a relevant document appears anywhere in the top k results. Mean reciprocal rank rewards placing the first relevant result as high as possible. Generation quality is harder to measure automatically, and exact-match or F1 against reference answers correlates only loosely with human judgment. Reporting token cost alongside quality is essential whenever a system claims an efficiency gain.
